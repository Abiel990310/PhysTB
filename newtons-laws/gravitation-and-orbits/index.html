Gravitation and orbits

The same inverse-square law as electrostatics, with one sign and no shielding — and the orbital results that follow from it.

By the end of this chapter you can

  • Apply Newton's law of gravitation and relate it to mg
  • Derive orbital speed and period from circular motion
  • Use gravitational potential energy with the correct zero point
  • Compute escape velocity from energy conservation

F=Gm1m2r2F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} F=Gr2m1​m2​​

The same 1r2\tfrac{1}{r^2}r21​ shape as Coulomb’s law in chapter 8.1, and for the same geometric reason — influence spread over a sphere thins as the sphere grows. Two differences matter:

  • Gravity has one sign. Mass is always positive and the force is always attractive. There is no gravitational repulsion and therefore no shielding — you cannot build a gravitational Faraday cage.
  • It is vastly weaker, by around 103610^{36}1036. It dominates at astronomical scale only because charge cancels and mass does not.

Where mgmgmg comes from

Near the surface of a planet, rrr barely changes, so the whole GMr2\tfrac{GM}{r^2}r2GM​ factor is effectively constant:

g=GMR2g = \frac{GM}{R^2} g=R2GM​

The second line is the important one and it reappears below: the derivative of −GMr-\tfrac{GM}{r}−rGM​ is +GMr2+\tfrac{GM}{r^2}+r2GM​, which is why gravitational potential energy has that form.

mgmgmg is therefore not a separate law — it is GMmr2\tfrac{GMm}{r^2}r2GMm​ with the constants collected, valid only while the height change is small compared with the planet’s radius. At orbital altitude it is not.

Pitfall

Astronauts in orbit are not beyond gravity. At the ISS’s altitude ggg is still about 89%89\%89% of its surface value.

They are weightless because they are in free fall — falling around the planet rather than into it. “Zero gravity” is a misnomer for “zero normal force”, and the distinction is a standard exam question.

Orbits

For a circular orbit, gravity supplies exactly the centripetal requirement:

GMmr2=mv2r⟹v=GMr\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} r2GMm​=rmv2​⟹v=rGM​​

The satellite’s own mass cancels — every object at a given radius orbits at the same speed, which is why a space station and a bolt floating beside it stay together.

Note the counterintuitive consequence: a higher orbit is a slower one, since v∝1rv \propto \tfrac{1}{\sqrt r}v∝r​1​. To catch something ahead of you in the same orbit, you must slow down and drop to a lower, faster one.

The period follows from circumference over speed:

T=2πrv=2πr3GMT = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}} T=v2πr​=2πGMr3​​

That verified line is Kepler’s third law — T2∝r3T^2 \propto r^3T2∝r3 — derived from Newton’s law in one step. Kepler found it by fitting data for decades; it falls out of the inverse square in a line, which is the single best advertisement for the theory.

Potential energy, and the zero point

U=−GMmrU = -\frac{GMm}{r} U=−rGMm​

The minus sign is not decoration and the chapter’s most-asked question is what it means.

The zero is at infinity. That is a choice, and it is the only convenient one for an inverse-square force: putting U=0U = 0U=0 at the surface would make it depend on which planet you are standing on. With zero at infinity, bound systems have negative total energy and free ones have positive — which turns out to be the useful classification.

And that is the check from chapter 3.1 again: F=−dUdrF = -\tfrac{dU}{dr}F=−drdU​, so the derivative of −GMmr-\tfrac{GMm}{r}−rGMm​ must be the force magnitude, and it is.

U=mghU = mghU=mgh is the near-surface approximation of this, with the zero moved to the ground — which is fine as long as h≪Rh \ll Rh≪R.

Escape velocity

Escaping means reaching infinity with at least zero speed left. Total energy must therefore be ≥0\ge 0≥0:

12mv2−GMmr≥0⟹vesc=2GMR\tfrac12 mv^2 - \frac{GMm}{r} \ge 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} 21​mv2−rGMm​≥0⟹vesc​=R2GM​​

That verified ratio is a result worth carrying: escape velocity is 2\sqrt22​ times orbital speed at the same radius. About 40%40\%40% more speed, not twice as much, which is a smaller margin than most people guess.

Escape velocity is also independent of the escaping object’s mass and of the direction it is launched — energy conservation cares about neither.
